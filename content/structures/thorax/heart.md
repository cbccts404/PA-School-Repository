anatomy:
  location: >-
    Middle [[mediastinum]], within the [[pericardium]], lying obliquely with about two-thirds to the left of the
    midline. The **apex** points anteriorly, inferiorly and to the left, at about the **left 5th intercostal space in
    the midclavicular line**.
  parts:
    - "**Right atrium**: receives the [[superior-vena-cava]], inferior vena cava and coronary sinus. Contains the SA node (near the SVC opening), the fossa ovalis, and the AV node (in the interatrial septum near the coronary sinus)"
    - "**Right ventricle**: trabeculae carneae, septomarginal trabecula (moderator band carrying the right bundle branch), [[tricuspid-valve]] inflow, [[pulmonary-valve]] outflow via the conus arteriosus (infundibulum)"
    - "**Left atrium**: receives four pulmonary veins. The most **posterior** chamber, directly in front of the [[esophagus]]"
    - "**Left ventricle**: thickest wall. [[mitral-valve]] inflow, [[aortic-valve]] outflow. Forms the apex"
    - "Interatrial and interventricular septa (the upper interventricular septum is membranous)"
    - "Conduction system: see [[cardiac-conduction-system]]"
  relations: >-
    **Surfaces.** Anterior (sternocostal): mainly the **right ventricle**. Diaphragmatic (inferior): mainly the left
    ventricle, with part of the right ventricle. Base (posterior): mainly the **left atrium**. **Borders on the PA chest
    X-ray.** Right border: SVC (above) and right atrium. Left border, from top to bottom: aortic knob (arch),
    pulmonary trunk, left atrial appendage (auricle), left ventricle. The right ventricle does not form a border on a PA film.
  bloodSupply: >-
    [[right-coronary-artery]] and [[left-coronary-artery]] (via the [[anterior-interventricular-artery]] and
    [[circumflex-artery]]). The coronary arteries fill mainly during **diastole**.
  venousDrainage: Cardiac veins → coronary sinus (in the posterior atrioventricular groove) → right atrium. Small anterior cardiac veins drain directly into the right atrium.
  innervation: >-
    Cardiac plexus. **Sympathetic** (upper thoracic segments, about T1–T4/T5): increases heart rate and contractility;
    carries visceral pain, which is referred to the chest, left arm and jaw. **Parasympathetic** (vagus): slows the
    heart rate.
clinical:
  - title: Penetrating cardiac injury
    highYield: true
    mechanism: The **right ventricle** forms most of the anterior surface, so it's the chamber most often injured by anterior stab wounds.
    presentation: Hemopericardium with **cardiac tamponade** (see [[pericardium]]) or hemorrhagic shock.
  - title: Left atrial enlargement
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Mitral stenosis or regurgitation, or long-standing atrial fibrillation.
    presentation: >-
      The enlarged posterior chamber can compress the **esophagus** (dysphagia) or, rarely, the left recurrent laryngeal
      nerve (hoarseness, **Ortner syndrome**). Predisposes to **atrial fibrillation** and thrombus in the left atrial
      appendage.
  - title: Referred cardiac pain (angina / MI)
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Visceral afferents travel with sympathetic fibers to upper thoracic spinal segments, which also receive somatic input from the chest wall and medial arm.
    presentation: Pressure-like retrosternal pain radiating to the left arm (T1–T2 dermatomes), jaw or epigastrium. Atypical presentations are more common in women, older adults and people with diabetes.
pance:
  - "Apex / PMI: **left 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line**. A laterally displaced PMI suggests LV dilation."
  - "Anterior surface = **right ventricle**; posterior (base) = **left atrium**."
  - "PA CXR left border (top → bottom): aortic knob, pulmonary trunk, left atrial appendage, left ventricle."
  - "Coronary perfusion occurs mainly in **diastole**; tachycardia shortens diastole."
  - "Enlarged left atrium → dysphagia (esophagus) ± hoarseness (Ortner syndrome); TEE images the LA best because it sits right behind the esophagus."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    **Cardiothoracic ratio** on a PA film: greater than about 0.5 suggests cardiomegaly (AP films exaggerate heart size).
    LA enlargement: a double density behind the right heart, a straightened left heart border, and a widened carina. RV
    enlargement: filling of the retrosternal space on the lateral view.
  ct: Cardiac CT and coronary CT angiography show coronary anatomy and calcium; CT also shows pericardial disease.
  mri: Cardiac MRI is the reference standard for ventricular volumes and function and for tissue characterization (late gadolinium enhancement in infarction and cardiomyopathy).
  ultrasound: >-
    **Echocardiography** is first-line for chambers, walls, valves and function. Standard transthoracic windows:
    **parasternal long and short axis**, **apical four-chamber**, **subcostal**, suprasternal. **TEE** (transesophageal)
    gives better views of the posterior structures: left atrium and appendage (thrombus), mitral valve, aorta.
  keyViews:
    - "PA and lateral CXR"
    - "Transthoracic echo: parasternal long/short axis, apical 4-chamber, subcostal (FAST view for pericardial fluid)"
exam:
  landmarks: >-
    Surface projection: right border from the 3rd to the 6th right costal cartilage about 1–2 cm from the sternum; left
    border from the 2nd left intercostal space to the apex (5th intercostal space, midclavicular line).
    {{verify: surface projection points vary slightly between texts}}
  palpation: >-
    **Point of maximal impulse (PMI)** at the apex: normally a small (about 1–2 cm) tap. Displacement or enlargement
    suggests LV dilation. A **parasternal heave** suggests RV enlargement. **Thrills** are palpable murmurs.
  testing: >-
    Auscultate at the four classic areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) and Erb's point, with the diaphragm and
    the bell, supine and in the left lateral decubitus position (mitral), and sitting forward (aortic regurgitation). See
    the valve entries.
quiz:
  - stem: A 25-year-old man has a stab wound just left of the sternum at the 4th intercostal space. Which cardiac chamber is most likely injured?
    choices:
      - Right atrium
      - Right ventricle
      - Left atrium
      - Left ventricle
      - Interatrial septum
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **right ventricle** forms most of the sternocostal (anterior) surface of the heart, so it's the chamber most at
      risk from anterior penetrating wounds.
  - stem: A patient with long-standing mitral stenosis develops difficulty swallowing solids. Which chamber is compressing the esophagus?
    choices:
      - Right atrium
      - Right ventricle
      - Left atrium
      - Left ventricle
      - Coronary sinus
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The **left atrium** is the most posterior chamber, lying directly in front of the esophagus. Mitral stenosis
      enlarges it, which can indent the esophagus.
  - stem: Which structure forms most of the right border of the heart on a PA chest radiograph?
    choices:
      - Right ventricle
      - Right atrium
      - Left atrium
      - Ascending aorta
      - Inferior vena cava
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **right atrium** forms the right heart border, with the SVC above it. The right ventricle is anterior and
      doesn't form a border on the PA view.
  - stem: Where is the normal apical impulse (PMI) located in an adult?
    choices:
      - Right 2nd intercostal space at the sternal border
      - Left 2nd intercostal space at the sternal border
      - Left 4th intercostal space at the sternal border
      - Left 5th intercostal space in the midclavicular line
      - Left 7th intercostal space in the anterior axillary line
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      The apex beat is normally at the **left 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line**. Displacement laterally or
      inferiorly suggests left ventricular enlargement.
  - stem: On a PA chest radiograph, which structures form the left heart border from top to bottom?
    choices:
      - Left ventricle, left atrium, pulmonary trunk, aortic knob
      - SVC, right atrium, right ventricle
      - Aortic knob, pulmonary trunk, left atrial appendage, left ventricle
      - Aortic knob, right ventricle, left ventricle
      - Pulmonary trunk, left atrium, right ventricle
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      The left border, from top to bottom, is the **aortic knob, pulmonary trunk, left atrial appendage and left
      ventricle**. The right border is the SVC and right atrium. The right ventricle forms the anterior surface and
      doesn't form a border on a PA film.
