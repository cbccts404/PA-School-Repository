name: Mitral valve
type: organ
region: thorax
subregion: Heart & pericardium
taName: Valva atrioventricularis sinistra
aka: [Bicuspid valve, Left atrioventricular valve]
summary: Two-leaflet valve between the left atrium and left ventricle, anchored by chordae tendineae to two papillary muscles. Mitral stenosis, regurgitation and prolapse are heard best at the apex.
tags: [heart, valves, murmurs]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the left atrium and left ventricle, posterior to the sternum at about the level of the left 4th costal cartilage. {{verify}}
  parts:
    - "**Anterior (aortic) leaflet** and **posterior (mural) leaflet**"
    - "**Chordae tendineae** attach the leaflets to the **anterolateral** and **posteromedial papillary muscles**, preventing prolapse into the atrium during systole"
    - "Annulus: part of the fibrous skeleton of the heart"
  relations: >-
    The anterior leaflet is continuous with the [[aortic-valve]]. The left atrium lies behind it, directly in front of
    the [[esophagus]]. The circumflex artery and coronary sinus run in the adjacent AV groove.
  bloodSupply: >-
    Papillary muscles: the **anterolateral** muscle usually has a **dual** supply (LAD and circumflex); the
    **posteromedial** muscle usually has a **single** supply (the posterior interventricular artery, from the RCA in
    right dominance), so it's **more vulnerable to rupture** in inferior MI.
  notes: "**Auscultation area**: the **apex**, left 5th intercostal space in the midclavicular line; the left lateral decubitus position accentuates mitral murmurs (especially stenosis, with the bell)."
clinical:
  - title: Mitral stenosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Almost always from **rheumatic heart disease** (commissural fusion). Rarely from annular calcification.
    presentation: >-
      Dyspnea, orthopnea, hemoptysis, and **atrial fibrillation** (with risk of LA thrombus and stroke). A loud S1, an
      **opening snap**, then a low-pitched **mid-diastolic rumble** at the apex. A shorter S2–opening snap interval
      means more severe stenosis. The **enlarged left atrium** can cause dysphagia or hoarseness (Ortner syndrome).
  - title: Mitral regurgitation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Primary: mitral valve prolapse, endocarditis, rheumatic disease. Secondary (functional): LV dilation stretching the
      annulus. **Acute**: **papillary muscle rupture** after MI (typically about 2–7 days after, posteromedial muscle, inferior MI) or
      chordal rupture.
    presentation: >-
      **Holosystolic, blowing murmur at the apex radiating to the axilla**. Acute severe MR: sudden pulmonary edema and
      cardiogenic shock (the murmur may be soft).
  - title: Mitral valve prolapse
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Myxomatous degeneration with billowing of the leaflets into the left atrium in systole. Associated with Marfan and Ehlers–Danlos syndromes.
    presentation: >-
      **Mid-systolic click** followed by a late systolic murmur. Maneuvers that **decrease preload** (standing, Valsalva)
      make the click **earlier** and the murmur **longer**; squatting has the opposite effect.
pance:
  - "MS: **opening snap + mid-diastolic rumble** at the apex; rheumatic; AFib; LA enlargement."
  - "MR: **holosystolic** murmur at the apex **radiating to the axilla**."
  - "MVP: **mid-systolic click**; standing/Valsalva → earlier click, longer murmur."
  - "Post-MI new holosystolic murmur + pulmonary edema → **papillary muscle rupture** (posteromedial, inferior MI)."
imaging:
  xray: >-
    MS: LA enlargement (straightened left heart border, double density, splayed carina), pulmonary venous hypertension
    (upper-lobe vascular redistribution, Kerley B lines).
  mri: Quantifies MR severity and LV volumes when echo is inconclusive.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Echocardiography**: MS shows thickened leaflets with commissural fusion and doming of the anterior leaflet ("hockey
    stick" appearance), and valve area by planimetry or pressure half-time. MR shows the regurgitant jet (color Doppler)
    and its mechanism (prolapse, flail leaflet, ruptured chordae or papillary muscle). **TEE** is best for detailed
    mitral anatomy and **LA appendage thrombus**.
  keyViews:
    - "Parasternal long axis, apical 4-chamber (color Doppler)"
    - "TEE for surgical/percutaneous planning and LA appendage thrombus"
exam:
  landmarks: "**Mitral area**: the apex, left 5th intercostal space in the midclavicular line."
  palpation: Laterally displaced, diffuse apical impulse in chronic MR (LV dilation). A tapping apex (palpable S1) in MS.
  testing: >-
    Listen at the apex with the **bell** in the **left lateral decubitus** position for the MS rumble. Use the diaphragm
    for the MR murmur and follow it toward the axilla. Stand and squat the patient to evaluate MVP.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 45-year-old woman who immigrated from a country with limited healthcare has dyspnea and palpitations. An ECG
      shows atrial fibrillation. There is an opening snap followed by a low-pitched diastolic rumble at the apex. What
      is the most likely underlying cause?
    choices:
      - Myxomatous degeneration
      - Rheumatic heart disease
      - Bicuspid aortic valve
      - Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
      - Infective endocarditis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Mitral stenosis** (opening snap and diastolic rumble) is almost always caused by **rheumatic heart disease**.
      The enlarged left atrium predisposes to atrial fibrillation.
  - stem: >-
      Four days after an inferior STEMI, a patient develops sudden dyspnea, pulmonary edema and a new holosystolic
      murmur at the apex. Which structure most likely failed?
    choices:
      - Anterolateral papillary muscle
      - Posteromedial papillary muscle
      - Interventricular septum
      - Free wall of the left ventricle
      - Aortic valve cusp
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **posteromedial papillary muscle** usually has a single blood supply (the posterior interventricular artery,
      from the RCA in right dominance), so it's more likely to rupture after an **inferior MI**, causing acute MR.
  - stem: A young woman has a mid-systolic click followed by a late systolic murmur. What happens to the click when she stands up from squatting?
    choices:
      - It occurs later in systole
      - It occurs earlier in systole
      - It disappears completely
      - It becomes a diastolic sound
      - No change
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      Standing **decreases preload** (smaller LV volume), so the prolapse occurs **earlier**, moving the click earlier and
      lengthening the murmur.
  - stem: Which papillary muscle of the left ventricle usually has a dual blood supply from the LAD and circumflex?
    choices:
      - Anterolateral papillary muscle
      - Posteromedial papillary muscle
      - Septal papillary muscle
      - Anterior papillary muscle of the right ventricle
    answer: A
    explanation: >-
      The **anterolateral** papillary muscle usually has a **dual** supply (LAD and circumflex). The **posteromedial**
      muscle usually depends on a single artery (the posterior interventricular), so it is more vulnerable to rupture
      after inferior MI.
  - stem: Which murmur is characteristic of chronic mitral regurgitation?
    choices:
      - Crescendo–decrescendo systolic murmur at the right upper sternal border radiating to the carotids
      - Holosystolic blowing murmur at the apex radiating to the axilla
      - Early diastolic decrescendo murmur at the left sternal border
      - Opening snap followed by a mid-diastolic rumble at the apex
      - Holosystolic murmur at the left lower sternal border that increases with inspiration
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      MR causes a **holosystolic, blowing murmur at the apex radiating to the axilla**. The other choices describe
      aortic stenosis, aortic regurgitation, mitral stenosis and tricuspid regurgitation.
