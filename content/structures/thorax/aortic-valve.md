status: draft
anatomy:
  location: At the outflow of the left ventricle, posterior to the left side of the sternum at about the level of the 3rd intercostal space. {{verify}}
  parts:
    - "Three semilunar cusps: **right, left and posterior (non-coronary)**"
    - "**Aortic sinuses (of Valsalva)**: dilations above each cusp. The **right coronary artery** arises from the right sinus and the **left coronary artery** from the left sinus"
    - "Normally tricuspid. A **bicuspid** aortic valve is the most common congenital heart anomaly"
  relations: Continuous with the anterior leaflet of the [[mitral-valve]] (aortomitral continuity). The membranous septum and His bundle lie just below.
  bloodSupply: Avascular cusps (nourished by diffusion). The coronary ostia sit in the sinuses just above the cusps.
  notes: >-
    **Auscultation area**: the **2nd right intercostal space at the right sternal border**. Aortic regurgitation is
    often best heard at the left sternal border (Erb's point, 3rd left intercostal space), with the patient leaning
    forward in held expiration.
clinical:
  - title: Aortic stenosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Age-related calcific** degeneration (older adults), a **bicuspid valve** (earlier presentation, often 40s–60s),
      or rheumatic disease (usually with mitral involvement).
    presentation: >-
      Symptoms: **syncope, angina and dyspnea (heart failure)**, classically exertional. **Crescendo–decrescendo
      systolic ejection murmur** at the right 2nd intercostal space **radiating to the carotids**. **Pulsus parvus et
      tardus** (weak, delayed carotid upstroke). A soft or absent A2 and a late-peaking murmur indicate severe stenosis.
      Symptomatic severe AS is an indication for valve replacement.
  - title: Aortic regurgitation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Leaflet disease (bicuspid valve, endocarditis, rheumatic) or **aortic root dilation** (hypertension, Marfan
      syndrome, aortic dissection, syphilitic aortitis, ankylosing spondylitis).
    presentation: >-
      **Early diastolic, blowing, decrescendo murmur** at the left sternal border, best with the patient sitting forward
      in held expiration. **Wide pulse pressure** with bounding pulses ("water hammer"/Corrigan pulse, de Musset head
      bobbing, Quincke capillary pulsations). An **Austin Flint** mid-diastolic rumble may be heard at the apex. Acute
      AR (dissection, endocarditis) causes sudden pulmonary edema and shock.
  - title: Infective endocarditis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Bacterial colonization of the valve, more likely with abnormal or prosthetic valves.
    presentation: Fever, a new regurgitant murmur, embolic phenomena (splinter hemorrhages, Janeway lesions), and immunologic signs (Osler nodes, Roth spots). Diagnosed with blood cultures and echocardiography (modified Duke criteria).
pance:
  - "AS: **syncope, angina, dyspnea**; crescendo–decrescendo systolic murmur at the **right 2nd ICS → carotids**; pulsus parvus et tardus."
  - "AR: **early diastolic decrescendo** murmur at the left sternal border; **wide pulse pressure**, bounding pulses."
  - "**Bicuspid aortic valve**: most common congenital heart defect; associated with **coarctation** and **Turner syndrome**; aortic root dilation."
  - "Handgrip increases AR (↑ afterload) and decreases AS; Valsalva and standing decrease the AS murmur (↓ preload)."
imaging:
  xray: Post-stenotic dilation of the ascending aorta; valve calcification (better on lateral). LV enlargement in chronic AR.
  ct: Aortic valve calcium scoring (helps grade AS); CT for sizing before transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR); aortic root dimensions.
  mri: Quantifies regurgitant volume and aortic dimensions.
  ultrasound: >-
    **Echocardiography** is the key test: valve morphology (bicuspid vs tricuspid), severity of AS (peak velocity, mean
    gradient, valve area) and AR (jet width, vena contracta), LV size and function, and aortic root size. TEE for
    endocarditis (vegetations, abscess) and better morphology.
  keyViews:
    - "Parasternal long axis (LVOT, root) and short axis (cusp number: the 'Mercedes-Benz' sign of a normal trileaflet valve)"
    - "Apical 5-chamber with continuous-wave Doppler for gradients"
exam:
  landmarks: "**Aortic area**: right 2nd intercostal space at the sternal border."
  palpation: Carotid pulse (slow-rising and weak in AS; bounding and collapsing in AR). A systolic thrill may be palpable at the base in severe AS.
  testing: >-
    Auscultate with the diaphragm at the aortic area and along the left sternal border. For AR, have the patient
    **sit up, lean forward and exhale fully**. Measure BP for pulse pressure. Dynamic maneuvers: **handgrip**
    (increases afterload) intensifies regurgitant murmurs; **Valsalva/standing** (decreases preload) softens the AS
    murmur.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 78-year-old man has exertional chest pain and two episodes of syncope. There is a harsh crescendo–decrescendo
      systolic murmur at the right upper sternal border radiating to both carotids, and the carotid upstroke is weak
      and delayed. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
      - Mitral regurgitation
      - Aortic stenosis
      - Aortic regurgitation
      - Ventricular septal defect
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **Aortic stenosis**: exertional syncope and angina, an ejection murmur at the aortic area radiating to the
      carotids, and **pulsus parvus et tardus**. In an older patient, calcific degeneration is the usual cause.
  - stem: >-
      A 25-year-old tall man with long fingers and lens dislocation has a blowing early diastolic murmur at the left
      sternal border and a BP of 150/45 mmHg. What is the mechanism of his murmur?
    choices:
      - Calcific aortic stenosis
      - Aortic root dilation causing aortic regurgitation
      - Mitral valve prolapse
      - Pulmonic stenosis
      - Coarctation of the aorta
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Marfan syndrome** causes **aortic root dilation**, which pulls the cusps apart and causes **aortic regurgitation**
      (early diastolic decrescendo murmur, wide pulse pressure). Mitral valve prolapse is also common in Marfan but gives
      a systolic click.
  - stem: Where is the aortic valve area for auscultation?
    choices:
      - Left 2nd intercostal space at the sternal border
      - Right 2nd intercostal space at the sternal border
      - Left 4th intercostal space at the sternal border
      - Left 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line
      - Right 4th intercostal space at the sternal border
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The **aortic area** is the **right 2nd intercostal space** at the sternal border. The pulmonic area is the left
      2nd, the tricuspid area the left lower sternal border, and the mitral area the apex.
  - stem: A girl with Turner syndrome is most likely to have which valve abnormality?
    choices:
      - Mitral valve prolapse
      - Tricuspid atresia
      - Bicuspid aortic valve
      - Pulmonary stenosis
      - Ebstein anomaly
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      A **bicuspid aortic valve**, the most common congenital heart anomaly, is associated with **Turner syndrome** and
      **coarctation of the aorta**, and with aortic root dilation. It can cause aortic stenosis earlier in life.
  - stem: How does sustained handgrip affect the murmurs of aortic stenosis and aortic regurgitation?
    choices:
      - Both murmurs get louder
      - Both murmurs get softer
      - AS gets louder; AR gets softer
      - AR gets louder; AS gets softer
      - Neither murmur changes
    answer: D
    explanation: >-
      Handgrip **raises afterload**, which increases regurgitant flow (**AR louder**) and reduces the gradient across
      the aortic valve (**AS softer**). Valsalva and standing reduce preload and soften the AS murmur.
