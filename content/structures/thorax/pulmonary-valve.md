name: Pulmonary valve
type: organ
region: thorax
subregion: Heart & pericardium
taName: Valva trunci pulmonalis
aka: [Pulmonic valve]
summary: Three-cusped semilunar valve between the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk. It is the most anterior valve, and the physiologic splitting of S2 is heard best over it.
tags: [heart, valves, murmurs]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: false
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: At the top of the conus arteriosus (infundibulum) of the right ventricle, the most anterior and superior of the valves, behind the left 3rd costal cartilage near the sternum. {{verify}}
  parts:
    - "Three semilunar cusps: **anterior, right and left**"
    - "Pulmonary sinuses above each cusp (no coronary arteries arise from them)"
  relations: Opens into the [[pulmonary-arteries|pulmonary trunk]], which lies anterior and to the left of the ascending aorta.
  notes: >-
    **Auscultation area**: the **2nd left intercostal space at the left sternal border**. **Physiologic splitting of
    S2**: on inspiration, increased venous return to the right heart delays pulmonic valve closure (P2), so A2 and P2
    separate. This is heard best at the pulmonic area.
clinical:
  - title: Pulmonic stenosis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Usually **congenital**. Associated with **Noonan syndrome**, tetralogy of Fallot, congenital rubella, and **carcinoid heart disease**.
    presentation: >-
      Crescendo–decrescendo **systolic ejection murmur** at the left upper sternal border that increases with
      inspiration, sometimes preceded by an ejection click. Wide splitting of S2 (delayed P2). RV heave in severe
      stenosis.
  - title: Abnormal splitting of S2
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      **Wide split** (splitting persists but widens with inspiration): delayed RV emptying (RBBB, pulmonic stenosis).
      **Fixed split** (no change with respiration): **atrial septal defect**. **Paradoxical split** (splits on
      expiration): delayed LV emptying (LBBB, severe aortic stenosis).
    presentation: Heard at the pulmonic area; timing with the respiratory cycle distinguishes them.
  - title: Pulmonic regurgitation
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Pulmonary hypertension (dilated annulus) or after repair of tetralogy of Fallot.
    presentation: Early diastolic decrescendo murmur at the left upper sternal border (the **Graham Steell murmur** in pulmonary hypertension).
pance:
  - "Pulmonic area: **left 2nd intercostal space**; best place to assess S2 splitting."
  - "**Fixed split S2 = ASD**; paradoxical split = LBBB or severe AS; wide split = RBBB or pulmonic stenosis."
  - "Pulmonic stenosis ↔ **Noonan syndrome**, tetralogy of Fallot, carcinoid."
imaging:
  xray: Post-stenotic dilation of the pulmonary trunk (prominent pulmonary artery segment on the left heart border) in valvular pulmonic stenosis.
  mri: Cardiac MRI quantifies pulmonary regurgitation and RV volumes, especially after tetralogy repair.
  ultrasound: Parasternal short axis at the great-artery level shows the valve and measures the gradient across it (continuous-wave Doppler).
  keyViews:
    - "Parasternal short axis (aortic valve level) with Doppler across the RV outflow tract"
exam:
  landmarks: "**Pulmonic area**: left 2nd intercostal space at the sternal border (just below the sternal angle, to the left)."
  palpation: A palpable P2 suggests pulmonary hypertension. A thrill may accompany significant stenosis.
  testing: Listen to S2 with the diaphragm during quiet inspiration and expiration to assess splitting.
quiz:
  - stem: A 30-year-old woman has a systolic ejection murmur at the left upper sternal border and a split S2 that does not vary with respiration. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    choices:
      - Ventricular septal defect
      - Atrial septal defect
      - Patent ductus arteriosus
      - Left bundle branch block
      - Aortic stenosis
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      A **fixed split S2** is characteristic of **ASD**. The systolic murmur comes from increased flow across the
      pulmonic valve, not from the defect itself.
  - stem: Where is the pulmonic valve best auscultated?
    choices:
      - Right 2nd intercostal space
      - Left 2nd intercostal space
      - Left 4th intercostal space
      - Apex
      - Right 5th intercostal space
    answer: B
    explanation: The **pulmonic area** is the **left 2nd intercostal space** at the sternal border.
  - stem: Paradoxical splitting of S2 is most likely associated with which condition?
    choices:
      - Right bundle branch block
      - Atrial septal defect
      - Left bundle branch block
      - Pulmonic stenosis
      - Normal inspiration
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      **LBBB** (and severe AS) delays aortic valve closure, so A2 follows P2 and the split appears on **expiration** and
      narrows on inspiration.
