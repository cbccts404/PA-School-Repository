name: Tricuspid valve

region: thorax

taName: Valva atrioventricularis dextra
aka: [Right atrioventricular valve]
summary: Three-leaflet valve between the right atrium and right ventricle. Tricuspid regurgitation, including from IV drug–associated endocarditis, is its key clinical problem.
tags: [heart, valves, murmurs]
systems: [cardiovascular]
highYield: true
status: draft
anatomy:
  location: Between the right atrium and right ventricle, behind the sternum at about the level of the 4th intercostal space. {{verify}}
  parts:
    - "Three leaflets: **anterior, posterior and septal**"
    - "Chordae tendineae attaching to the anterior, posterior and septal papillary muscles"
  relations: The septal leaflet lies near the membranous septum and the AV conduction tissue.
  notes: "**Auscultation area**: the **left lower sternal border** (4th–5th intercostal space)."
clinical:
  - title: Tricuspid regurgitation
    highYield: true
    mechanism: >-
      Usually **functional** (RV dilation from pulmonary hypertension or left heart failure). Primary causes include
      **infective endocarditis in people who inject drugs** (commonly *Staphylococcus aureus*), **carcinoid heart
      disease**, **Ebstein anomaly**, rheumatic disease, and pacemaker/ICD leads.
    presentation: >-
      **Holosystolic murmur at the left lower sternal border that increases with inspiration** (**Carvallo sign**).
      Prominent **v waves** in the JVP, a **pulsatile liver**, peripheral edema, ascites.
  - title: Right-sided endocarditis
    highYield: true
    mechanism: Injection drug use; *S. aureus* is the most common organism.
    presentation: Fever and **septic pulmonary emboli** (multiple peripheral nodules, often cavitating) with a TR murmur. Left-sided embolic signs are usually absent.
  - title: Ebstein anomaly
    highYield: false
    mechanism: Apical displacement of the septal and posterior leaflets, "atrializing" part of the RV. Associated with **maternal lithium** exposure.
    presentation: Cyanosis, TR, right heart failure and arrhythmias (associated with WPW).
pance:
  - "TR: holosystolic murmur at the **left lower sternal border**, **louder on inspiration** (Carvallo sign); giant v waves in the JVP."
  - "Injection drug use + fever + murmur → **tricuspid endocarditis** (*S. aureus*) with septic pulmonary emboli."
  - "Ebstein anomaly ↔ **lithium** exposure in pregnancy."
  - "Right-sided murmurs increase with **inspiration** (↑ venous return)."
imaging:
  xray: Right atrial enlargement (prominent right heart border). Septic emboli in endocarditis show as multiple peripheral nodules.
  ct: Septic pulmonary emboli (peripheral, cavitating nodules, feeding vessel sign).
  ultrasound: >-
    Echo shows leaflet morphology, vegetations, regurgitant jet severity, RV size and function, and estimates **pulmonary
    artery systolic pressure** from the TR jet velocity. Ebstein anomaly shows apical displacement of the septal leaflet.
  keyViews:
    - "Apical 4-chamber and RV-focused views; subcostal (IVC size)"
exam:
  landmarks: "**Tricuspid area**: left lower sternal border, 4th–5th intercostal space."
  palpation: Parasternal (RV) heave. A pulsatile, enlarged liver in severe TR.
  testing: >-
    Listen at the left lower sternal border during **inspiration and expiration**: right-sided murmurs intensify with
    inspiration. Inspect the JVP for large v (cv) waves.
quiz:
  - stem: >-
      A 28-year-old man who injects heroin has fever and a holosystolic murmur at the left lower sternal border that
      increases with inspiration. Chest CT shows multiple peripheral cavitating nodules. What is the most likely
      organism?
    choices:
      - Streptococcus viridans
      - Staphylococcus aureus
      - Enterococcus faecalis
      - Streptococcus bovis (gallolyticus)
      - Candida albicans
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Tricuspid valve endocarditis** in people who inject drugs is most often caused by ***S. aureus***. The
      cavitating nodules are **septic pulmonary emboli**.
  - stem: A holosystolic murmur that gets louder during inspiration is most consistent with which lesion?
    choices:
      - Mitral regurgitation
      - Aortic stenosis
      - Tricuspid regurgitation
      - Ventricular septal defect
      - Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
    answer: C
    explanation: >-
      Inspiration increases venous return to the right heart, intensifying **right-sided** murmurs (**Carvallo sign** in
      TR). MR doesn't change or softens with inspiration.
  - stem: A newborn whose mother took lithium during pregnancy has cyanosis and an enlarged right atrium. Which valve abnormality is expected?
    choices:
      - Bicuspid aortic valve
      - Apical displacement of the tricuspid leaflets
      - Mitral valve prolapse
      - Pulmonary valve atresia
      - Quadricuspid aortic valve
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      **Ebstein anomaly**, apical displacement of the septal and posterior **tricuspid** leaflets, is associated with
      prenatal **lithium** exposure.
  - stem: What are the three leaflets of the tricuspid valve?
    choices:
      - Right, left and posterior
      - Anterior, posterior and septal
      - Anterior, posterior and lateral
      - Aortic, mural and septal
      - Superior, inferior and septal
    answer: B
    explanation: >-
      The tricuspid valve has **anterior, posterior and septal** leaflets. The septal leaflet lies near the membranous
      septum and AV conduction tissue. The aortic valve has right, left and posterior (non-coronary) cusps.
  - stem: Which jugular venous finding is typical of significant tricuspid regurgitation?
    choices:
      - Cannon a waves
      - Absent a waves
      - Kussmaul sign only
      - Steep y descent only
      - Large (giant) v waves
    answer: E
    explanation: >-
      Regurgitant flow into the right atrium during systole produces **prominent (giant) v waves** in the JVP, often
      with a **pulsatile liver**. Cannon a waves occur with AV dissociation.
